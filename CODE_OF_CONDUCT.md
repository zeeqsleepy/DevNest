# Code of Conduct

## The point of this document

Most people never need a code of conduct, because most people behave reasonably. It exists so
that when someone does not, the response is predictable rather than improvised, and so that
everyone knows in advance what to expect.

## Expected behaviour

- Assume good faith. A blunt comment is usually a busy person, not a hostile one, and a
  contributor's first language may not be the one this project is written in.
- Aim criticism at the code. "This function does not handle the empty case" is a review. "You
  clearly did not think about this" is not.
- Accept that maintainers say no. Scope decisions are documented in `docs/prd.md` and are not
  personal.
- Remember that everyone here is doing this voluntarily, on their own time.
- Be patient with people who are new to the codebase, to Go, or to open source.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks, in any project space.
- Discriminatory language or jokes based on race, gender, sexuality, disability, religion,
  nationality, age, or appearance.
- Unwelcome attention of a sexual nature.
- Publishing someone's private information without their consent.
- Sustained disruption: derailing threads, repeatedly reopening settled decisions, or demanding
  attention from maintainers.
- Deliberately introducing malicious code, or misrepresenting the origin or licensing of a
  contribution.

## Scope

Applies to every project space: issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, code
comments, and any space where someone is representing the project.

## Reporting

Report a problem to the maintainers through GitHub's private reporting, or directly via the
address on the repository's organisation profile. Include what happened, where, when, and any
links.

Reports are handled privately. Your identity is not shared with the person reported without your
agreement.

If a maintainer is the problem, report to a different maintainer.

## Enforcement

Maintainers respond in proportion to what happened.

1. **A private word.** For a one-off, usually a misjudged comment. An explanation of what was
   wrong and why.
2. **A warning.** For a repeat, or something more serious. On record, with a clear statement of
   what will happen next time.
3. **A temporary ban.** Removed from project spaces for a set period.
4. **A permanent ban.** For sustained behaviour after a warning, or for anything severe enough on
   its own: harassment, malicious contributions, threats.

Enforcement decisions are explained to the person affected. Appeals go to the maintainers in
writing and are considered once.

## Maintainers

Maintainers are held to this document more strictly than anyone else, not less. Someone with the
power to merge and to ban has more capacity to make this an unpleasant place, and the same
standards apply to them with less benefit of the doubt.
